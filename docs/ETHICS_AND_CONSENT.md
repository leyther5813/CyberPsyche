# CyberPsyche Ethics and Consent

**Project:** A Decision-Process Laboratory for Social-Engineering Resilience
**Status:** Draft template — investigator and institutional review required before recruitment.

## 1. Study purpose

This study investigates whether a short behavior-targeted learning intervention improves independent verification when people encounter simulated social-engineering messages, including when the context or communication channel changes.

The study uses fictional, controlled scenarios. It is not a diagnostic assessment and does not determine whether a participant has a psychological condition.

## 2. Who may participate

The planned pilot is intended for adults aged 18 or older who voluntarily agree to participate.

Before recruitment, the investigator must confirm applicable institutional requirements, including whether ethics committee or institutional review approval is required. Do not recruit or collect study data until required approvals and consent materials are finalized.

## 3. What participation involves

The planned session may include:

1. Reading study information and providing consent.
2. Completing a brief onboarding activity.
3. Reviewing simulated messages and inspecting available evidence.
4. Making decisions and optionally providing a short justification.
5. Receiving a brief learning intervention.
6. Completing retest and transfer scenarios.
7. Viewing a debrief and information about privacy and withdrawal.

The final duration, number of scenarios, data collected, and intervention allocation must be confirmed in the approved study protocol before this document is presented to participants.

## 4. Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participation is voluntary. Participants may stop participating without penalty.

Explain clearly how withdrawal works, including whether data already collected can be identified and deleted, the point after which deletion may no longer be possible, and whom to contact. Do not promise deletion beyond what the system and approved protocol can support.

## 5. Foreseeable risks and safeguards

Some scenarios may resemble urgent, manipulative, or deceptive messages encountered in everyday life. Participants may experience mild discomfort or uncertainty.

Safeguards should include:

- Use fictional organizations, people, domains, and contact details.
- Do not ask for passwords, OTPs, payment information, private messages, or other real secrets.
- Do not include functional malicious links or real attack payloads.
- Make clear that scenarios are simulations and provide a debrief.
- Avoid diagnostic labels, blame, or shaming participants for decisions.
- Allow participants to stop if uncomfortable.

## 6. Data collected and privacy

The investigator must specify the exact data collected before recruitment. The planned system may record decisions, evidence interactions, timestamps, response latency, scenario assignments, intervention allocation, and consent status.

Before approval, document:

- Which direct identifiers, if any, are collected.
- How consent records are separated from behavioral data.
- How participant records are pseudonymized.
- Who can access identifiable and pseudonymous data.
- Where data are stored and how they are protected.
- The retention period and deletion process.
- Whether data will be exported, shared, or published.
- Any limits to confidentiality.

Participants should be told not to enter real personal or confidential information into free-text fields.

## 7. Benefits and compensation

Do not promise a direct personal benefit. The study may help improve understanding of verification behavior and the design of educational interventions.

State any compensation, reimbursement, or lack thereof in the approved participant-facing version. Do not leave this ambiguous in the final form.

## 8. Consent statement — draft

Please use this only after review and adaptation by the responsible investigator.

- [ ] I confirm that I am at least 18 years old.
- [ ] I have read and understood the study information.
- [ ] I understand that the messages and scenarios are simulations.
- [ ] I understand what information will be collected and how it will be used.
- [ ] I understand that participation is voluntary and that I may stop, subject to the stated limits on withdrawal and deletion.
- [ ] I have had an opportunity to ask questions.
- [ ] I agree to participate in this study.

The application must record consent only after the participant takes an affirmative action. Declining consent must prevent entry into the research session.

## 9. Debrief requirements

At the end of participation, explain the study purpose, the intervention comparison, and why scenarios may have included misleading or ambiguous cues. Clarify that performance is not a diagnosis or a measure of personal worth. Provide investigator and ethics-contact details approved for the study.

## 10. Required investigator completion

Before use, complete and verify:

- Investigator and institution details.
- Study contact and independent ethics contact, where applicable.
- Approved study duration and procedures.
- Exact data fields, storage, retention, and deletion policy.
- Compensation details.
- Withdrawal and data-removal procedure.
- Ethics approval or documented determination that approval is not required.
- Final participant-facing language and accessibility review.

**Do not present this draft as an approved consent form.**
